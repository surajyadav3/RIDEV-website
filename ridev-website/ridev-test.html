      Rider questions

      Straight answers.

      Is the battery swap really free?

      Yes — included in the rent, with no cap on how many times you swap. Energy is the most unpredictable cost a delivery rider carries, and making it free is what turns a rental into a genuinely fixed weekly number.

      What if the bike breaks down mid-shift?

      Bring it to your hub, or call and we arrange recovery. Repairs, servicing and parts are on us. If the bike has to stay back, you leave on a replacement the same day and your subscription carries over.

      Do I need a down payment, a loan or a credit check?

      None of the three. One week's rent and a refundable deposit. No financing, no EMI, no ownership risk — stop riding and you stop paying.

      Can I use it for Zomato, Swiggy, Blinkit, Zepto or Delhivery work?

      Yes — that is what the fleet is built for. A large share of our bikes are configured for delivery duty, and we work directly with logistics operators including Delhivery.

      Is insurance included?

      Yes. The bike stays insured for your whole subscription and RIDEV manages the claim. Insurance repairs run through the same workflow as everything else.

        The model

        A rider cannot buy a ₹1.2 lakh vehicle. A fleet operator can buy six thousand.

        India's gig workforce runs on two-wheelers it cannot afford to own, service or replace. RIDEV takes the asset, the energy and the downtime off the rider and prices the whole thing as one weekly number.

        Growth trajectory

        Fleet on road, July 2024 to August 2026.

        Ten vehicles at launch. 1,400 by the Shark Tank pitch. 6,702 today, across five cities.

        Markets

        Depth in seven cities before breadth in twenty.

        RIDEV concentrates fleet into a small number of dense hubs rather than spreading it thin. That is what makes free swapping and in-house maintenance physically possible — and the hub is the unit that gets replicated when a new city opens.

          Where the next vehicles go.

          Pune and Bengaluru

          Both cities are already configured on the RIDEV platform. Hub selection and initial fleet allocation are the remaining steps.

          Hub density in live cities

          Chennai, Gurugram and Mumbai each run a single hub against a growing fleet. Adding hubs shortens rider travel to swap and repair — the direct lever on utilisation.

          The Delhivery deployment is the template: dedicated vehicles, hub-side swapping and maintenance SLAs sold to logistics operators rather than one rider at a time.
